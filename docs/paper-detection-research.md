# Paper detection: lighting and document-scanner research

Research date: 2026-10-05. Two research agents inspected primary OpenCV documentation, a document-boundary scoring paper, and detector source in five GitHub repositories. The repositories were not executed or benchmarked against Tracefinity's photos; their methods are comparisons, not evidence that another detector is more accurate here.

## Observed failure and decision

The first failed upload had cool-lit paper rejected by the HSV saturation gate. A narrow Lab retry recovered it. The next upload exposed a separate issue: the fixed brightness thresholds 200/190/180 discarded much of a visible sheet after morphological opening. Lower-exposure variants also sometimes returned a smaller bright interior rectangle, so a fallback that runs only when detection returns `None` cannot fix the whole failure pattern.

Keep the existing neutral-first/cool-second color gates, tool-disconnecting opening, threshold candidates, geometry checks, and optional U2-Net masking. Before contour validation, estimate the 95th-percentile brightness of the **positive, color-selected, opened grayscale pixels**, excluding artificial masking zeros. Scale the three thresholds by `min(1, P95 / 200)`. Using the existing highest threshold as the reference avoids adding an independent reference constant. Bright scenes with P95 at least 200 retain their original thresholds; dim scenes adapt before an undersized rectangle can be accepted. Empty eligible samples produce no brightness candidate.

The percentile policy is a local, regression-backed heuristic—not an OpenCV-prescribed algorithm or a universal exposure model. A /210 prototype also passed the initial probes; the final /200 policy passed the same 36 photo/resolution/exposure combinations before integration.

## Best-practice findings

- **Separate candidate generation from validation.** OpenCV binary thresholding retains only intensities strictly greater than the threshold. Otsu minimizes histogram-class variance, not paper-corner or physical-scale error. Global Otsu on a color-masked image is influenced by artificial zeros; even selected-pixel Otsu can classify paper shadows or metal as the foreground class. See [OpenCV thresholding](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d7/d4d/tutorial_py_thresholding.html).
- **Correct the demonstrated intensity gate, not the entire image.** Scaling comparison thresholds avoids another full-image normalization/remapping pass. Local adaptive thresholding and CLAHE are not automatic upgrades: adaptive mean-minus-C can admit uniform dark regions, and contrast enhancement can amplify wood texture. See [adaptive thresholding](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d7/d1b/group__imgproc__misc.html#ga72b913f352e4a1b1b397736707afcde3) and [histogram equalization/CLAHE](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d5/daf/tutorial_py_histogram_equalization.html).
- **Color is a prior, not material identification.** White paper can be saturated in HSV under colored illumination; gray metal also passes a neutrality test. OpenCV's uint8 Lab coordinates are offset around 128, unlike float Lab. Whole-scene gray-world balancing assumes average-gray scene content and can misinterpret a wood-dominated bench. See [color conversions](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/de/d25/imgproc_color_conversions.html) and [GrayworldWB](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d7/d71/classcv_1_1xphoto_1_1GrayworldWB.html#details).
- **Morphology and hulls have geometric limits.** Opening disconnects narrow protrusions, but wide tools may survive. Closing and convex hulls bridge occlusion gaps but can also connect unrelated bright objects. `minAreaRect` encloses all supplied extremes; it does not recover the true boundary after an outward protrusion. See [morphology](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d9/d61/tutorial_py_morphological_ops.html) and [contour features](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/dd/d49/tutorial_py_contour_features.html).
- **Boundary evidence is stronger than a plausible box.** If a labelled failure remains, corroborate existing candidates with tolerant, distributed side support and inside/outside appearance contrast from the original image. Do not require uninterrupted edges where tools cross the sheet. [Tropin et al., Contours and Contrasts](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.02615v2#S3.SS1) combines boundary consistency and color contrast; its dataset assumes a document exists, so it does not establish no-paper safety for this application.
- **Measure corner and calibration error, not just detection success.** Enlarging the paper reference makes measured tools too small; accepting a bright interior makes them too large. A high overlap score can conceal a displaced corner. Perspective, lens distortion, and objects above the reference plane remain separate limitations. See [OpenCV homography theory](https://docs.opencv.org/4.13.0/d9/dab/tutorial_homography.html#tutorial_homography_Basic_theory).

## Local verification evidence

- Four independent real-photo fixtures with manually labelled sheet corners, tested at three image resolutions and exposures 1.0, 0.85 and 0.65: **36 correlated variants**, not 36 independent photographs.
- Existing synthetic bright-wood tests also cover neutral/cool sheets, silver protrusions and dark tool occlusion. Dark-background tests exercise external saliency selecting either the tool or sheets covering different frame fractions.
- No-paper cases cover an empty black frame and a real wood/tools crop without the sheet. These are limited negative evidence, not a general false-positive-rate estimate.
- The integrated detector was exercised through the live dev `POST /api/upload` with real U2-Net on all four original-resolution photos. Maximum coordinate errors against hand labels were 3 px for the new drill photo, 6 px for the previous cool-lit photo, 1 px for the caliper photo, and 0 px for the pliers photo. Smoke sessions were removed afterward.
- A Chromium smoke upload through `http://localhost:4001` reached the actual corner editor; its displayed handles matched the new photo's four paper corners. The temporary browser session was removed afterward.
- Full verification after integration: 1,123 backend tests passed; 399 frontend tests passed under Node 22 with two workers; `make lint` passed with warnings.

## Remaining limits and deferred alternatives

The upper brightness tail can be dominated by broad shiny tools or reflections. Sparse visible paper, colored backgrounds, strong local shadows, warm/mixed casts, multiple white rectangles and appreciable perspective remain difficult. Digital dimming does not reproduce camera noise, gamma, clipping or mixed illumination. A minimum-area rectangle is not a projective quadrilateral.

No Otsu sweep, CLAHE pipeline, Hough/LSD rewrite, extra runtime dependency or learned page model was added. If another labelled failure shows unsupported sides or hull expansion, evaluate one independent tolerant side-support check on the existing candidates, with additional clutter/no-paper captures. Do not silently infer reliable physical calibration from a geometrically plausible rectangle.
